What is a stakeholder? A stakeholder is anyone who has a real stake in how a situation unfolds: they may gain or lose, be helped or harmed, depending on the decision made. Stakeholders include obvious parties (the person asking you to act, the person affected) and non-obvious ones (bystanders, families, institutions, future parties).
Why tension matters here: Ethical tension isn't just about values in the abstract; it's about people. Each stakeholder in a dilemma experiences the tension differently. Some are on the side of accountability; some are on the side of compassion. Identifying who's caught in the tension (and on which side) is the first move in navigating it responsibly.
